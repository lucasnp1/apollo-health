Growth hormone raises blood sugar. That is not a side effect, it is part of what the hormone does. Anything that raises growth hormone, whether it is GH itself, MK-677, ipamorelin or CJC-1295, can raise fasting glucose and blunt insulin. Plenty of people find this out on their first panel after starting.

## The three numbers to read together

- **Fasting glucose** often moves first. Under 100 mg/dL (5.6 mmol/L) is normal; 100 to 125 is prediabetes on paper.
- **Fasting insulin** shows how hard the pancreas is working to keep glucose there. Labs accept up to about 25 µIU/mL; lower figures, under 8 or so, are commonly quoted as better. Normal glucose with high insulin often means early insulin resistance.
- **HbA1c** averages about three months of blood sugar. Under 5.7 percent is normal. On testosterone it can read a little low, because red cells turn over faster and donations replace old cells with new, so a good HbA1c does not cancel out a high fasting glucose.

## Why it happens

Growth hormone tells the liver to release glucose and tells muscle and fat to use less of it. MK-677 does this around the clock, which is why it is a common cause of a surprise prediabetic reading in someone lean and training hard. Body fat, poor sleep and high cortisol add to it.

## Things to raise with your doctor

- Every compound you take, including peptides, with the start date and any change. A doctor cannot read your glucose without knowing what is raising growth hormone.
- Fasting glucose, fasting insulin and HbA1c from the same draw, and the change since your last test.
- Whether HbA1c is reliable for you, given testosterone and any blood donations.
- Fasting glucose of 126 mg/dL (7 mmol/L) or more on two tests is the diabetes threshold, whatever caused it. That one belongs with your doctor straight away.

## Keep the line

HbA1c takes about three months to reflect a change, so one panel says little. Keeping the three numbers on one line, next to the dates you started or changed a compound, is what makes the pattern visible.
