Estradiol is the estrogen men make from testosterone. On TRT it rises with the dose, and that is mostly a good thing: it matters for bone, joints, HDL, mood and libido. A higher number on its own is not automatically a problem.

## The ratio and the number

Some people read estradiol next to testosterone as a ratio: testosterone in ng/dL divided by estradiol in pg/mL. A ratio of roughly 15 to 30 is commonly quoted on forums, but it is not a clinical guideline. A testosterone of 900 with an estradiol of 45 is a ratio of 20; a testosterone of 400 with an estradiol of 45 is a ratio of 9. The same estradiol reads differently next to a different testosterone.

The assay matters too. The sensitive (LC-MS/MS) estradiol test reads men's levels more accurately than the standard immunoassay, which can give false highs.

## What high and low can feel like

- **Higher:** water retention, a puffy face, sore or puffy nipples, moodiness.
- **Very low:** dry or aching joints, flat mood, low libido, poor erections, fatigue.

These signs overlap with plenty of other causes, so they are worth writing down and bringing to your doctor next to the number. Very low estradiol on TRT most often goes with an aromatase inhibitor.

## Why it moves

- **Injection size and frequency.** Big, infrequent injections aromatise more at the peak than the same weekly dose spread out.
- **Body fat.** Aromatase lives in fat, so leaner men run lower estradiol on the same dose.
- **Alcohol.** It can raise estradiol for days, which shows up if the draw follows a heavy weekend.
- **Dose.** Estradiol follows testosterone. A low dose with a low estradiol usually reflects the dose.

## Things to raise with your doctor

- Whether your lab used the sensitive estradiol assay.
- Any symptoms from the lists above, and when they started relative to a dose change.
- How your injection schedule might affect your estradiol.
- Whether an aromatase inhibitor makes sense for you at all. That is a medical decision, not one to make from a forum.

## Keep it on one line

Estradiol swings with timing and assay more than most markers. Same lab, same draw timing, and a line showing every result next to the testosterone it came with makes it much easier for you and your doctor to see whether anything actually changed.
